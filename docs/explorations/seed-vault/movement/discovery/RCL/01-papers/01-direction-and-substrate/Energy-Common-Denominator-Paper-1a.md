## Evidence Intake 001 — Power Syntax versus Capacity Syntax

The restored broad vocabulary requires a strict distinction between transfer and storage. $P=ef$ is the established instantaneous power syntax, while $\mathcal K=\kappa S\Psi^2$ is a candidate capacity classification whose variables must be bound separately. The universal symbol $\Psi$ is therefore called **activation**, not flow: it may be a displacement, field amplitude, invariant conversion scale, gradient, or rate. **Flow** remains reserved here for the power-conjugate variable $f$.

The thermal rows require two distinct reversible boundary-port interpretations:

$$
P_Q=T\dot S_{\mathrm e}=\dot Q_{\mathrm{rev}},
\qquad
P_B=(T-T_0)\dot S_{\mathrm e}.
$$

The first is heat-energy transfer at boundary temperature $T$; the second is the associated exergy transfer relative to environment $T_0$. Internal entropy generation is not a boundary flow and remains in the entropy and exergy balances.

**Grammar consequence:** no squared-capacity expression may be inferred from $P=ef$ without an independently specified storage functional or constitutive relation.

## Evidence Intake 003 — Thermal RC Analogy Is Not a Power Bond

The widespread engineering pair $(\Delta T,\dot Q)$ is useful for thermal-circuit calculation because

$$
\dot Q=C_{\mathrm{th}}\dot T,
\qquad
\Delta T=R_{\mathrm{th}}\dot Q.
$$

It is not a power-conjugate pair: $\Delta T\,\dot Q$ has units K·W, not W. The qualified grammar must mark it as a **pseudo-bond or signal-flow analogy**.

The energetic pair is $(T,\dot S_{\mathrm e})$, and the near-environment exergy pair is $(T-T_0,\dot S_{\mathrm e})$. This distinction is mandatory for dimensional validity, energy closure, and the Energy–Entropy–Exergy ledgers.
