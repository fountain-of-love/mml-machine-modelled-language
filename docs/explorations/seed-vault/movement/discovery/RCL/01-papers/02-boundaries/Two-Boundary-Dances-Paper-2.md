## Central Question

> Which fundamental tensions determine whether stored physical capacity can become active and remain useful?

## Core Proposition

Two tensions recur beneath the five-domain grammar:

$$
\boxed{
\textbf{Containment versus Flow}
}
$$

and


$$
\boxed{
\textbf{Coherence versus Dispersion}
}
$$

The first tension determines whether energy can become active. The second determines whether that activity becomes enduring capacity or dissipative loss.

## The First Tension: Containment ↔ Flow

A substrate must contain energy before it can express anything. Yet perfect containment produces no movement, work, sound, heat transfer, or circulation.

$$
\boxed{
\text{Containment}
\longleftrightarrow
\text{Flow}
}
$$

| Containment side | Flow side |
| --- | --- |
| Substrate | Activation |
| Storage | Transfer |
| Potential | Motion |
| Compliance | Flux |
| Boundary | Permeability |
| State | Process |
| Memory | Expression |
| Capacity | Work |

> How can a system remain sufficiently bounded to hold energy, yet sufficiently open to let energy act?

## The Second Tension: Coherence ↔ Dispersion

Once energy flows, a second question appears: does the flow accumulate coherently, or does it disperse into less recoverable forms?

$$
\boxed{
\text{Coherence}
\longleftrightarrow
\text{Dispersion}
}
$$

| Coherence side | Dispersion side |
| --- | --- |
| Resonance | Dissipation |
| Synchronization | Decoherence |
| Order | Entropy generation |
| Amplification | Attenuation |
| Recoverability | Irreversibility |
| Exergy retention | Exergy destruction |
| Signal | Noise |
| Rhythm | Friction |

> How can energy accumulate into an organized mode without its movement generating the losses that dissolve that organization?

## How the Tensions Unfold

The second tension unfolds from the first:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Containment}
\longleftrightarrow
\text{Flow}
\longleftrightarrow
\text{Coherence}
\longleftrightarrow
\text{Dispersion}
}
$$

1. A substrate **contains** energy.
2. A difference produces **flow**.
3. Matching timing and structure produce **coherence**.
4. Resistance and irreversible coupling produce **dispersion**.
5. The remaining exergy determines what can return as useful action.

> **The first dance determines whether capacity can move. The second determines whether movement can remain capacity.**

## Mediation by the Active Triad

The Energy–Entropy–Exergy triad sits between the poles:

| Triad member | Role in containment ↔ flow | Role in coherence ↔ dispersion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Energy** | Moves between containment and flow | Supplies both resonance and dissipation |
| **Entropy** | Marks irreversible transfer across boundaries | Measures dispersion and loss of coherence |
| **Exergy** | Measures capacity available for action | Measures what remains useful after dissipation |

$$
\boxed{\text{Energy}=\text{what crosses the tension}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Entropy}=\text{what makes the crossing irreversible}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Exergy}=\text{what survives the crossing as useful capacity}}
$$

The triad is therefore discovered through the tensions rather than imposed upon them.

## Equilibrium as a Cross-Cutting Regime

Near-versus-far-from-equilibrium behavior changes the shape of both dances but is not proposed as a third primary tension.

Near equilibrium:

- containment is stable;
- flows are proportional to gradients;
- resonance can remain linear;
- entropy production is approximately quadratic;
- cross-domain transposals are comparatively direct.

Far from equilibrium:

- the substrate changes under the flow;
- coefficients become state-dependent;
- resonance may saturate;
- entropy production becomes nonlinear;
- new regimes emerge.

$$
\boxed{
\text{near equilibrium}
\Rightarrow
\text{linear, quadratic, transposable}
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
\text{far from equilibrium}
\Rightarrow
\text{nonlinear, adaptive, emergent}
}
$$

## Evidence Intake 001 — Qualified Activation and Coherence

**Activation** includes a gradient, displacement, field amplitude, rate, or movement capable of changing a substrate's state. It does not replace the narrower bond-graph flow variable.

**Coherence** is broader than resonance. Resonance is a dynamical frequency-response phenomenon defined relative to natural modes, damping, forcing, and boundaries. Phase coherence, spatial mode overlap, geometric matching, timing, and coupling efficiency are related organization mechanisms but require their own operational variables. Contact is a boundary or coupling condition, not resonance by itself.

A generic $G_r$ is admissible only for a specifically defined resonant response. Broader matching factors require different symbols until equivalence is demonstrated. Near equilibrium and near resonance remain independent coordinates, and no absolute frequency—including 432 Hz or 440 Hz—has universal resonant priority.
